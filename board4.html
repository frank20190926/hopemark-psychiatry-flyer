Treatment-Resistant Depression

More options. Renewed hope.
What Is Treatment-Resistant Depression?

When depression has not improved enough after at least two antidepressants taken at an adequate dose for an adequate length of time, it may be treatment-resistant depression (TRD).

Symptoms such as persistent sadness, low energy, and loss of interest can continue to affect everyday life.

How Common Is It?

A U.S. study estimated that approximately one-third of adults receiving medication for major depression had treatment-resistant depression.

How Hopemark Health Can Help

Our team reviews each patient’s symptoms and treatment history to build a personalized plan that may include novel therapies, medication management, and psychotherapy.

Deep TMS

Deep Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation

What it is and how it works: A noninvasive treatment that uses magnetic pulses to stimulate brain networks involved in mood and OCD. FDA-cleared for depression and as an additional treatment for OCD.

Potential benefits: May reduce persistent depression symptoms. An OCD-specific treatment protocol may reduce obsessive thoughts and compulsive behaviors.

Before TMSPotential response after TMS
Educational visualization. Results vary.

Spravato®

Esketamine Nasal Spray

What it is and how it works: An FDA-approved prescription nasal spray for adults with treatment-resistant depression. It acts on NMDA receptors in the brain’s glutamate system.

Potential benefits: May reduce depression symptoms, with improvement observed as early as approximately 24 hours in clinical trials. It can be used with or without an oral antidepressant for TRD.

IV Ketamine

What it is and how it works: Low-dose ketamine delivered through an IV. It acts on NMDA receptors in the brain’s glutamate system and is used off-label for depression.

Potential benefits: May relieve depression symptoms within hours for some patients when standard treatments have not provided enough relief. Additional treatments may be needed to maintain benefits.

*In a 2021 U.S. study, an estimated 30.9% of adults with medication-treated major depression had treatment-resistant depression. Source: Zhdanava et al., Journal of Clinical Psychiatry. 2021;82(2):20m13699.
